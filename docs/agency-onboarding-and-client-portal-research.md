# Agency Onboarding And Client Portal Research

Last updated: 2026-06-03

Purpose: define what Kreative Reflow should collect before a website or portal project starts, how the client portal should support delivery while work is ongoing, and how the agency should protect scope, schedule, payment, and client communication.

## Executive Summary

Strong agencies do not treat onboarding as a form. They treat it as a gate between sales and delivery. The project should not start until the agency has a signed agreement, confirmed scope, deposit/payment status, named decision makers, a complete brief, asset/access readiness, timeline constraints, and a communication plan.

The current Kreative Reflow portal already has the right foundations: authenticated access, project data, onboarding, assets, approvals, activity, invoices, and handoff. The next improvement is to turn those pieces into a stricter operating system:

- A project cannot enter `Active delivery` until required onboarding gates are complete.
- Client updates should be mostly asynchronous in the portal, supported by weekly email summaries.
- WhatsApp and phone should be allowed for urgency and relationship warmth, but not as the official source of truth.
- Change requests must be logged, classified, estimated, and approved before work starts.
- Dashboard updates should be generated from delivery actions wherever possible, not manually rewritten after every design task.

## What Mature Agency Onboarding Usually Includes

Research across agency onboarding guides and portal products shows a repeatable lifecycle:

1. Contract and payment are completed first.
2. Sales-to-delivery handoff captures what was promised.
3. Client onboarding questionnaire collects business, project, brand, content, technical, approval, and communication details.
4. Internal setup creates project workspace, roles, timeline, folders, reporting, and tasks.
5. Kickoff meeting confirms scope, timeline, responsibilities, communication cadence, and change process.
6. Client is shown how to use the portal for status, uploads, feedback, approvals, and requests.
7. First visible value is delivered within the first 10-14 days when possible.

Teamwork describes onboarding as the transition from signed contract to active delivery, with intake, kickoff, tool training, communication agreements, and early check-ins. AgencyAnalytics frames onboarding as contract/payment, internal kickoff, questionnaire, reporting setup, project management, launch meeting, and scope-creep prevention.

## Start Gate: Required Before Work Begins

These should be required before the project moves from `Onboarding` to `Active delivery`.

### Commercial Gate

- Signed proposal, contract, or engagement letter.
- Signed scope of work.
- Deposit or first invoice paid, if required.
- Billing contact and billing email.
- VAT/tax details where applicable.
- Payment schedule confirmed.
- Cancellation, pause, and late-payment rules acknowledged.

### Decision Gate

- Primary project contact.
- Backup contact.
- Final decision maker.
- People allowed to approve deliverables.
- Client-side turnaround expectation for feedback.
- What happens if feedback is late.
- Who can request billable changes.

### Scope Gate

- Project type: new website, redesign, client portal, dashboard, automation, SEO, maintenance, or mixed.
- In-scope deliverables.
- Out-of-scope items.
- Number of included revision rounds.
- Page list or feature list.
- Required integrations.
- Launch definition.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Phase 2 ideas that are not included now.

### Strategy Gate

- Business description.
- Target audience.
- Primary project goal.
- Secondary goals.
- Success metrics.
- Main call to action.
- Pain points with current workflow/site.
- Competitors or comparison sites.
- Brand positioning and tone.
- Services/products that matter most.

### Content And Asset Gate

- Logo files.
- Brand guidelines, colors, fonts.
- Photos and images.
- Page copy or copywriting responsibility.
- Team bios.
- Service descriptions.
- FAQs.
- Testimonials/reviews.
- Case studies/proof.
- Legal/compliance copy.
- Downloads or lead magnets.
- Missing content list with owner and due date.

### Technical Gate

- Current domain registrar.
- DNS access.
- Hosting access.
- CMS/admin access.
- Analytics access.
- Search Console access.
- Email/domain provider.
- Payment provider access, if relevant.
- Booking/calendar provider.
- CRM or form destination.
- Existing integrations.
- Backup/export of current website if redesigning.
- Redirect requirements.

### Compliance And Risk Gate

- POPIA/privacy-sensitive data collected.
- Consent/privacy wording needed.
- Cookie/analytics requirements.
- Industry-specific compliance constraints.
- Accessibility expectations.
- Security expectations.
- Data retention expectations.
- Credential handoff method.

POPIA matters here because client portals collect personal information and project records. POPIA Chapter 3 includes conditions around accountability, processing limitation, purpose specification, openness, security safeguards, and data subject participation.

## Recommended Onboarding Questionnaire

Keep the first client form focused. AgencyAnalytics suggests 15-20 questions; Teamwork suggests keeping intake short enough that it gets completed, while still capturing goals, success metrics, brand, assets, approvals, and communication.

Use grouped sections instead of one long form.

### 1. Project Basics

- Is this a new website, redesign, portal/dashboard, automation, SEO, maintenance, or mixed project?
- What is the main outcome you want from this project?
- What would make this project successful in your words?
- What are the top two actions visitors/users should take?
- What is the target launch date and why?
- Are there immovable dates, events, shutdown periods, or campaign deadlines?

### 2. Business And Audience

- What does your business do?
- Who is the ideal customer/user?
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- What makes your business different from competitors?
- Which products/services should the website emphasize first?
- What questions do prospects ask before buying/contacting you?

### 3. Current Website Or Workflow

- Do you have an existing website? If yes, URL.
- What works well on the current site?
- What is not working?
- What should stay?
- What should be removed?
- What data, pages, or content must be migrated?

### 4. Brand And Content

- Do you have brand guidelines?
- Upload logo files, fonts, colors, and brand assets.
- Upload photos, videos, brochures, service docs, and testimonials.
- Who is responsible for final copy?
- Which pages already have approved copy?
- Which pages still need copywriting?
- Are there words, claims, or visuals we must avoid?

### 5. Features And Integrations

- Which features are required for launch?
- Which features are nice-to-have or Phase 2?
- Do you need forms, booking, payments, downloads, login, dashboard, file uploads, or automations?
- What tools must connect to the site?
- Who owns each tool/account?

### 6. Access

- Who can provide domain/DNS access?
- Who can provide hosting/CMS access?
- Who can provide analytics/Search Console access?
- Who can provide booking/payment/CRM access?
- Should access be granted via invited user accounts instead of shared passwords?
- Are any credentials too sensitive for the portal?

### 7. Approval And Communication

- Who gives final approval?
- Who else should review work?
- What is your preferred update rhythm: weekly, twice weekly, milestone-only?
- Preferred channel for non-urgent updates: portal, email, WhatsApp, phone?
- Preferred channel for urgent blockers?
- Expected response time for approvals.
- Best days/times for meetings.

### 8. Scope Boundaries

- Which parts are definitely included?
- Which parts are definitely not included?
- How many revision rounds are included?
- Who may request billable changes?
- Do you agree that new pages, new features, direction changes after approval, and extra revision rounds require a change request?

## Contract And SOW Guardrails

The contract should not be a scary wall of legal language, but it must protect the agency and train the client how the project works.

Include:

- Parties, project name, and effective dates.
- Scope of work and deliverables.
- Out-of-scope list.
- Client responsibilities.
- Agency responsibilities.
- Timeline and dependencies.
- Payment schedule, deposits, late payment, suspension rights.
- Revision rounds and what counts as a revision.
- Change request process.
- Approval process and deemed approval period.
- Third-party costs: fonts, stock, hosting, plugins, domains.
- IP ownership and transfer conditions.
- Confidentiality.
- Data protection and POPIA/privacy handling.
- Portfolio/case-study permission.
- Warranty/bug-fix period.
- Maintenance/support boundary after launch.
- Termination/pause rules.

Nexos' public terms are a useful model: they define revision rounds as one consolidated set of changes, state that revisions refine the agreed direction rather than change project goals, and require additional fees for extra revisions or major direction changes. Their terms also require client review within a defined period and treat unreviewed work as deemed approved after that period.

The AIGA Standard Form of Agreement is also useful for design-service logic: when a request sits outside original scope, the designer should issue a change order describing additional time and cost, with approval before work proceeds.

## Scope Protection: Classify Every Request

Every client request during the project should be classified before action.

### 1. Fix / Defect

Definition: the deliverable does not match the approved scope, approved design, or technical acceptance criteria.

Action: fix at no extra charge.

Examples:

- Broken link.
- Layout bug.
- Form not submitting.
- Approved copy missing from a page.

### 2. Included Revision

Definition: refinement to a submitted deliverable within the included revision round.

Action: no extra charge while rounds remain; must be consolidated per round.

Examples:

- Adjust spacing.
- Swap one image.
- Refine wording on an existing section.
- Change button label.

### 3. Change Request

Definition: new scope, new direction, new feature, new page, new integration, major rework after approval, or extra revisions beyond the included rounds.

Action: estimate cost and time, then wait for approval before work starts.

Examples:

- Add a new service page not in the SOW.
- Change the approved homepage concept.
- Add e-commerce after a brochure website was scoped.
- Add client portal functionality to a website-only scope.
- Rewrite all copy after approval.

### 4. Maintenance Request

Definition: post-launch or retainer update.

Action: route to maintenance plan, support block, or separate quote.

Examples:

- Update team member bio.
- Add blog post.
- Swap prices.
- Add tracking code after launch.

## Change Request Workflow

Frank LTD's public change-control process is a good simple model: client raises request, agency checks whether it is in scope, performs impact assessment when out of scope, shares time/cost estimate, waits for approval, then schedules the change.

For Kreative Reflow:

1. Client submits request in portal.
2. System asks: page/feature affected, desired change, reason, urgency, deadline, screenshot/file.
3. Studio classifies: fix, included revision, change request, maintenance.
4. If included: attach to current revision round.
5. If out of scope: create impact assessment.
6. Show client cost, timeline impact, and whether launch date moves.
7. Client approves, declines, or parks for Phase 2.
8. Approved change becomes a tracked task/milestone.
9. Activity log records who requested, who approved, and when.

Do not let WhatsApp messages become invisible scope. If a request arrives by phone or WhatsApp, the studio should log it in the portal and send a written confirmation.

## Ongoing Client Communication

Recommended channel model:

### Portal

Source of truth for:

- Project status.
- Milestones.
- Client tasks.
- Uploads.
- Feedback.
- Approvals.
- Change requests.
- Meeting notes.
- Billing/handoff.

### Email

Use for:

- Weekly digest.
- Formal decisions.
- Meeting summaries.
- Invoice/payment notifications.
- New approval notifications.

### WhatsApp

Use for:

- Quick reminders.
- Urgent "please check portal" nudges.
- Relationship warmth.
- Simple scheduling confirmation.

Do not use WhatsApp as the official place for approvals, scope changes, sensitive data, or final decisions. If it happens there, capture it in the portal.

### Phone / Video Call

Use for:

- Kickoff.
- Strategy/discovery.
- Confusing feedback.
- Scope tension.
- Urgent blocker.
- Major decision.

After every call, create a portal note with decisions, action items, owner, and due date.

## Update Rhythm

For most Kreative Reflow website projects:

- Portal activity updates: automatic as work moves.
- Weekly client update: every Friday or agreed weekday.
- Milestone review meetings: at sitemap/wireframe, design concept, pre-launch QA, handoff.
- Urgent blocker updates: same business day.
- Client response expectation: 2-3 business days unless project requires faster.

Dashboard status should not be manually rewritten from scratch. It should update from events:

- Milestone status changed.
- Asset uploaded.
- Asset accepted/rejected.
- Deliverable sent for review.
- Approval submitted.
- Revision requested.
- Invoice marked due/paid.
- Handoff item completed.
- Change request approved.

## Dashboard Automation Model

The studio should update one internal delivery action; the client dashboard should react automatically.

Examples:

- Designer uploads homepage concept v1 -> portal creates review item, activity entry, client notification.
- Studio moves milestone to `Review` -> portal progress and "current milestone" update.
- Client approves homepage -> approval history logs decision, next milestone unlocks.
- Client requests revision -> revision round count increments, studio task created.
- Client uploads photos -> asset bucket count updates, studio review queue gets item.
- Invoice paid -> finance panel updates.

Recommended event table model:

- `project_events`: project_id, event_type, title, detail, actor, visibility, created_at.
- `deliverables`: title, version, status, due date, approval state.
- `change_requests`: request detail, classification, estimate, status, approval.
- `client_messages`: project_id, thread/context, visibility, source channel.
- `meeting_requests`: reason, preferred slots, status, agenda.
- `portal_tasks`: owner type, due date, client-visible status.

## Recommended Meeting Flow

Clients should be able to request a meeting in the portal, but not bypass the workflow.

Meeting request fields:

- Reason.
- Topic type: strategy, design review, technical access, billing, scope change, urgent blocker.
- Preferred dates/times.
- Attendees.
- Agenda notes.
- Related milestone/deliverable/request.

Rules:

- Minor clarification -> portal message first.
- Design review -> scheduled call with agenda.
- Scope change -> change request first, meeting if needed.
- Urgent blocker -> WhatsApp/phone allowed, then written portal note.

## Proposed Onboarding Gate For The Portal

The portal should show a clear checklist:

- Agreement signed.
- Deposit paid.
- Project brief complete.
- Approval contact confirmed.
- Brand assets uploaded.
- Required copy uploaded or assigned to studio.
- Technical access granted.
- Timeline constraints confirmed.
- Communication preferences confirmed.
- Kickoff completed.

Only then should the project move to `Active delivery`.

## Practical Scripts For Scope Control

### When a request is in scope

"Yes, that fits inside the current scope. I have logged it under this revision round and will update the portal once it is complete."

### When a request is a small paid change

"This is a good addition, but it is outside the approved scope. I will log it as a change request with the time/cost impact so you can decide whether to approve it now or park it for Phase 2."

### When feedback is scattered

"To keep the project moving cleanly, please add the full feedback list to the portal review item. That way we can treat it as one revision round and avoid missing anything from WhatsApp or email."

### When a client wants a call for every small edit

"A quick call is useful when direction is unclear. For smaller edits, please submit them in the portal so we can track what was requested, what changed, and whether it affects timeline or scope."
